The spread of Islam.

7 sourced chapters connect places, people, and periods through the 2026 research horizon. The interactive globe enhances this page when JavaScript is available.

  1. A community takes shape — Muhammad’s preaching in Mecca and the migration to Medina in 622 are central to the emergence of the Muslim community. By his death in 632, the movement had become a religious and political force in Arabia.
  2. Expansion beyond Arabia — Under Abu Bakr and Umar, the caliphate consolidated power in Arabia and expanded into lands previously governed by the Byzantine and Sasanian empires. Territorial expansion and the gradual adoption of Islam are distinct historical processes.
  3. Trade, pilgrimage, and learning — Trans-Saharan connections linked West African societies with North Africa. Mansa Musa’s pilgrimage through Cairo in 1324 and his patronage of scholarship illustrate the roles of rulers, travel, and learning in these networks.
  4. A traveller encounters connected communities — Ibn Battuta’s Rihla describes a world linked by pilgrimage, legal learning, hospitality, and Muslim courts. His account is evidence of connections between communities, not a precise map of when entire populations converted.
  5. Institutions in the city — Sinan’s Selimiye complex at Edirne brings together worship, education, and commercial space. Such institutions show how Muslim religious and social life became embedded in urban settings.
  6. Two billion Muslims in the measured population — Pew Research Center estimates that the global Muslim population grew from 1.7 billion in 2010 to 2.0 billion in 2020. Its analysis attributes much of the growth to a younger population and higher fertility. These figures measure population change, not simply conversion.
  7. A living history, with dated evidence — The project’s research horizon reaches 2026. Historical accounts and demographic findings retain their own dates. The 2020 population estimate is not relabelled as a 2026 count, and projections are not presented as observed facts.
