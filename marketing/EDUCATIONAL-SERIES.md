# IslamicLives — 24 educational content episodes

**Goal:** create evergreen learning posts that drive curiosity and source literacy without inventing unverified biographical facts. Use a named example only after a reviewer has confirmed each claim and its citation. Posts below are reusable prompts and copy; they do not assert religious facts about any particular person.

## Series A: How to read a life (6)
1. **Beyond the name:** “What changes when you ask when, where, life and source—not just who?” Exercise: select a profile, write four questions.
2. **What is a source?** “A story becomes more useful when you can identify where it comes from.” Exercise: find one cited passage.
3. **Different kinds of evidence:** “A Qur'anic passage, hadith report and historical chronicle aren't interchangeable.” Exercise: identify the label.
4. **What remains unknown:** “Not knowing is not a failure of learning.” Exercise: note one uncertainty.
5. **Chronology with humility:** “Ancient dating may vary; what difference does the range make?” Exercise: compare dates where documented.
6. **Reading a map responsibly:** “Geographic context is helpful. An illustrated atlas isn't proof of an exact journey.” Exercise: compare map label and source note.

## Series B: Places, journeys and context (6)
7. **A place can connect many lives:** find a shared location from two separately sourced profiles.
8. **Maps as questions:** what historical research would you need to establish a route?
9. **Why names change:** explore transliteration and alternative names without conflating identities.
10. **Cities of learning:** select a documented scholar profile and trace a referenced city.
11. **Before borders:** discuss why a modern country outline should not be projected backward as historical ownership.
12. **The atlas is a starting point:** use a chapter to open a source and record a follow-up reading.

## Series C: The people behind knowledge (6)
13. **Prophets in the Qur'an:** distinguish the actual Qur'anic reference from explanatory additions.
14. **Companions and transmission:** ask which collection or historical source supports a stated claim.
15. **Scholars:** ask what their documented contributions were rather than repeating honorifics.
16. **Scientists and builders:** connect work, place and time only where directly sourced.
17. **Travelers and historians:** distinguish eyewitness narrative from later retelling.
18. **Women in Islamic history:** responsibly invite research into well-sourced women's biographies; avoid asserting current project coverage.

## Series D: Build with care (6)
19. **One correction matters:** show how a precise citation fix improves an educational tool.
20. **A good historical issue:** copy the disputed sentence, provide evidence, state the proposed change.
21. **What automated checks cannot do:** passing tests does not verify theology or historical truth.
22. **Accessible history:** demonstrate keyboard and screen-reader requirements.
23. **Community review:** explain the difference between structural review, source-alignment review and qualified scholar review.
24. **Open development, responsible reuse:** explain why a publicly readable repository still needs a proper license.

## Repeatable weekly cadence
Monday: learning prompt; Tuesday: interface journey; Wednesday: sources and evidence; Thursday: community contribution; Friday: reflection and questions; weekend: optional educator guide.

## Five scripts for 20–40 second videos
### Video 1: Four questions
Text: “Can four questions change how you study history?” Voice: “When did they live? Where are they connected to? What does their life teach us to investigate? Which sources tell us about them? IslamicLives builds a learning journey around those four questions.” CTA: “Explore the project on GitHub.”

### Video 2: Follow the reference
Text: “Don't stop at the summary.” Voice: “A biography introduces a claim. A citation gives you somewhere to look. Learning goes further when you open the reference and study its context.” CTA: “Help improve source-linked learning.”

### Video 3: Maps can mislead
Text: “Is this ancient route exact?” Voice: “Not necessarily. Many places and journeys involve uncertainty. The IslamicLives atlas is illustrative—it helps orient learners without pretending to reconstruct exact historical routes.” CTA: “Review the atlas methodology.”

### Video 4: One small contribution
Text: “You don't have to rebuild the entire app.” Voice: “A more precise citation, an accessibility fix or a clearly stated uncertainty can make an educational project better.” CTA: “Read CONTRIBUTING.md.”

### Video 5: Why community matters
Text: “One idea can become a shared resource.” Voice: “A member of our ummah.build community helped start IslamicLives. Now the invitation is to keep improving it together—with care, evidence and respect for scholarship.” CTA: “Meet IslamicLives on GitHub.”

## Educational guardrails
No fabricated quotations, exact dates or claims about named figures. Check the Qur'anic text, hadith edition/grading and historiographic context before publication. Do not depict prophets. Never treat engagement as evidence of religious accuracy.
