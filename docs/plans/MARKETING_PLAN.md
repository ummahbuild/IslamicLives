# Islamic Lives marketing plan

## Positioning

Islamic Lives is an evidence-first way to explore people connected to the Qur’an, Sunnah, and Islamic history. Its differentiator is visible sourcing and honest uncertainty—not claims of exhaustive authority.

## Audiences

- Muslim families and students seeking a respectful starting point.
- Educators who need direct references and clear evidence labels.
- Curious readers exploring Islamic history across time and geography.
- Scholars and reviewers who can improve the project’s accuracy.

## Launch sequence

### Foundation

- Publish methodology, editorial limits, and correction policy before promotional campaigns.
- Recruit qualified reviewers and display only review scopes they explicitly approve.
- Prepare privacy disclosures, app-store metadata, support contact, and correction workflow.

### Web discovery

- Build focused pages around prophet reading guides, scholars, places, and historical periods.
- Submit the sitemap to search consoles and monitor indexing and broken links.
- Use short educational posts that link to the exact source-backed profile rather than summarising disputed material without context.

### Mobile launch

- Run a small TestFlight and Play internal-testing cohort with educators and families.
- Measure successful profile reads, source-link opens, saves/shares, crashes, and correction reports.
- Launch publicly only after accessibility, device, store-policy, and scholarly-review gates pass.

## Content channels

- X: concise source threads and release notes.
- LinkedIn: research process, education use cases, and ummah.build product work.
- TikTok and YouTube: short narrated journeys that show references on screen.
- GitHub: transparent data and engineering changes, issue templates, and contributor guidance.

## Guardrails

- Never market “complete Islamic history” or “all hadith” before corpus completeness is demonstrable.
- Never use AI-generated depictions of prophets.
- Do not optimise controversy, sectarian conflict, or sacred claims for engagement.
- Corrections and source improvements are a primary success signal, not a reputational failure.

## Initial success measures

- Profile completion and return visits.
- Source-link open rate.
- Search queries with no result.
- Correction turnaround time.
- Crash-free sessions and store rating after statistically meaningful volume.
