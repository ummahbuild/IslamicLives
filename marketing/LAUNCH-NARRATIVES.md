## Narrative 1 — The community is the story (flagship; LinkedIn / newsletter)
### A single community contribution can become a doorway to centuries of learning

There is a particular kind of progress we want to see in the Muslim technology ecosystem: someone in a community recognizes an opportunity for benefit, starts building, and others have a place to join them.

Today we're proud to introduce **IslamicLives**, a project brought forward through the initiative of one of our ummah.build community members.

Behind so many names in Islamic history are lives of struggle, faith, exploration, service, learning and human complexity. Those stories are often encountered in isolation: a passage in a lesson, a footnote in a book, a name on a map. IslamicLives is an attempt to make it easier to move between these connections.

The experience is organized around four questions: **When did this person live? Where are they associated with? What do we know of their life? What sources tell the story?**

The current working collection has **81 biographies** across prophets, other Qur'anic figures, Companions, scholars, scientists, travelers, rulers and builders. Readers can search, explore categories, discover a random life and save profiles for later. An interactive 3D atlas adds geographic context, explicitly as an **illustration rather than a claim of exact historical routes or borders**.

The part we're especially proud of is the humility built into the research method. Accounts derived from Qur'anic passages are distinguished from transmitted hadith and later history. Uncertainty should not disappear just because an interface is beautiful. This project is a starting point for learning and further checking, not an authoritative or complete religious reference.

For us, IslamicLives also points to a larger possibility: more Muslim community members building educational tools, and more readers, researchers and developers having a clear way to make those tools better.

The GitHub repository is public and invites carefully sourced corrections, accessibility fixes, testing and engineering contributions. An explicit open-source license, documented scholarly review and production-release testing are still on the roadmap.

**Explore IslamicLives:** https://github.com/ummahbuild/IslamicLives

May Allah make the work a means of beneficial knowledge and sincere collaboration.

## Narrative 2 — The learning problem (educator + parent)
### Knowing a name isn't the same as understanding a life

Many of us can recognize a name from Islamic history. Fewer of us can immediately place the story in time, identify its geographic context, or distinguish which details come from the Qur'an, hadith, or historical writing.

**IslamicLives** was designed around that gap.

Rather than presenting each figure as an isolated fact card, it organizes exploration around **When → Where → Life → Story**. The project brings source-linked profiles and an illustrative atlas into a connected learning experience. It currently includes 81 profiles and keeps its research limitations visible.

A useful way to learn together: choose one person, read the available account, open the source references, identify what is established and what is disputed, then write down one question for further study with a qualified teacher.

This is not a replacement for teachers, books or scholarly review. It's a public, growing project that can become more useful through careful contribution.

Explore the repository: https://github.com/ummahbuild/IslamicLives

## Narrative 3 — The technical achievement (developer community)
### Building an Islamic history experience that can be improved, not merely viewed

IslamicLives is more than a collection of static biographies. The repository documents a web and Expo experience with a shared generated content package, searchable and saved profiles, crawlable person pages, and a Three.js historical atlas.

But the architecture has a second goal: make research changes auditable. Source changes belong in generation scripts and must pass structured checks; uncertainty is treated as content, not a footnote to hide. Validation helps detect structural problems but cannot certify the accuracy of a historical or religious claim.

Current scope: 81 working profiles, 16 documented atlas chapters, sourced biographies, interactive discovery and repository-local editorial guidelines. The project still needs further qualified review, device and accessibility testing, and explicit licensing.

If you enjoy building educational technology with care, explore the issues and contribution rules:
https://github.com/ummahbuild/IslamicLives

## Narrative 4 — Open knowledge with responsibility (research audiences)
### Accuracy is a feature, not a badge

In historical education, it is tempting to make every story feel complete. But disputed dates, uncertain identities and differences between textual traditions do not disappear because an application draws a timeline.

IslamicLives aims to make those limits legible. The project separates kinds of evidence, surfaces uncertainty, and invites source corrections. It also clearly states what it cannot yet claim: exhaustive historical coverage, comprehensive hadith indexing or qualified scholarly review of most entries.

We hope this approach inspires a better standard for community-developed educational tools: inviting to explore, explicit about sources, open to correction, and respectful of scholarly expertise.

See the research-oriented project and how to contribute: https://github.com/ummahbuild/IslamicLives
