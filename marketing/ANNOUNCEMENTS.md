## Flagship announcement (ummah.build)

**Introducing IslamicLives — discover the lives that shaped Islamic history.**

Alhamdulillah, we're proud to share IslamicLives, a community-driven project made possible by the initiative of one of our ummah.build community members.

We wanted to make learning about people in the Qur'an, Sunnah, and Islamic history more exploratory, accessible, and grounded in sources. IslamicLives brings together biographies, references, and an interactive historical atlas to invite a simple journey: **When → Where → Life → Story**.

The working collection currently includes **81 profiles**—from the 25 prophets in the commonly taught list to Companions, scholars, scientists, travelers, rulers, and builders. Explore sources, discover related histories, search by category, save lives to revisit, and see illustrative geographic context through the 3D atlas.

The project takes an evidence-first approach: Qur'anic passages and transmitted reports are labeled separately, disputed details are not passed off as certainty, and the team openly documents what still needs review. This is an educational starting point, not a substitute for qualified scholarship.

Above all, this is what we hope ummah.build can enable: community members helping turn meaningful ideas into tools others can learn from and improve.

The GitHub repository is public and welcomes careful contributions—from source corrections and research to accessibility, mobile testing, and engineering. Licensing, scholarly review, and production release work remain in progress.

Explore and contribute: https://github.com/ummahbuild/IslamicLives

May Allah place barakah in knowledge that is beneficial, in those who preserve it, and in those who share it.

## LinkedIn (organization)
**IslamicLives: a community member's idea becoming a resource for learners.**

One of our favorite things about building ummah.build is seeing community members initiate projects with real potential for shared benefit.

Today we're introducing **IslamicLives**, a project for exploring figures in the Qur'an, Sunnah and Islamic history through a research-oriented learning journey: **When → Where → Life → Story**.

The working edition contains 81 source-linked biographies and an interactive historical atlas. It also offers category-based search, Saved Lives, shareable URLs and a web-and-Expo architecture using a shared content package.

The most important design choice is not a visual effect. It's the decision to make evidence and uncertainty visible. Qur'anic accounts and hadith reports receive distinct labels; editorial limits and gaps are documented; and qualified scholarly review remains a clear next step rather than a claim we make prematurely.

We are sharing the public GitHub project to invite careful collaboration across research, engineering, accessibility, education and peer review. We're proud of the community initiative behind this work and grateful to those helping improve it.

Explore the repository and contribution guide: https://github.com/ummahbuild/IslamicLives

## 90-word media description
IslamicLives is a community-driven educational project associated with ummah.build. It helps readers explore people in the Qur'an, Sunnah, and Islamic history through source-linked biographies and illustrative historical geography. Its working collection currently includes 81 profiles, with searchable categories, saved profiles, and a 3D chapter-based atlas. The project uses a shared web-and-Expo content architecture and emphasizes clear evidence labels and uncertainty notes. The GitHub repository is public and welcomes carefully sourced contributions. An explicit software license, qualified scholarly review, and public production-release checks remain pending.
